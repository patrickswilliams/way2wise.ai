Research

Code and Character

Revealing emotional self-awareness and self-regulation in software engineers through personalized AI interactions. The Pepperdine University study the EQ Agent grew from.

Abstract

As artificial intelligence increasingly automates the technical production of software, human capacity—specifically emotional intelligence (EQ) centered on emotional self-awareness and self-regulation—has become a more critical professional differentiator than ever before. This study found limited evidence of an existing AI-mediated EQ development model designed specifically for practicing software engineers and combining personalized reflection, contextual baselines, and qualitative feedback.

The qualitative case study involved ten software engineers over a four-week intervention, revealing that while AI serves as an effective “external mirror” to surface emotional blind spots, its efficacy depends heavily on established trust and personalized context.

These findings offer significant implications for scholarship and professional development practice by providing a framework for integrating personalized AI as an adjunct coach to prepare technical professionals for an automated future.

Four themes

  • Trust, privacy and the “Citation Paradox”Citations built trust for some engineers and caused overload for others. Fear of corporate monitoring could lead people to hold back the truth. 9 of 10 participants
  • Context first: personal “user guides”Engineers needed the AI to understand their real situation before it asked deep emotional questions. 7 of 10 participants
  • Modality and interface frictionFaster audio and visual maps helped. Mobile auto-scrolling, buffering and hidden controls got in the way. 7 of 10 participants
  • The shifting engineering roleAs AI automates coding, EQ, conflict resolution and self-management become the core engineering skills. 8 of 10 participants

How the study was done

Ten practicing software engineers took part over four weeks: short podcasts on EQ concepts, weekly reflection sessions with the EQ Agent, and a final 45-minute interview. Themes came from triangulating the AI reflection logs, the weekly reflections and the interviews. No personally identifiable information entered the AI systems, and the study followed Pepperdine University's Institutional Review Board protocol #25-07-2663.
